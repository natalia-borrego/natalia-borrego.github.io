How do ecological conditions shape social behavior?

I study when social strategies are beneficial, how animal societies organize themselves, and how individuals adjust their behavior to changing ecological and social conditions.

Comparative behavioral ecology

One species. Different worlds.

African lions are my primary comparative system. Studying the same social system across contrasting environments lets us ask which features of animal societies are stable—and which emerge from the ecological conditions in which animals live.

Namibia

Life at low density

Desert-adapted lions move through vast, resource-limited landscapes where opportunities to encounter groupmates, rivals, prey and people can be unpredictable.

Botswana

Behavior reconstructed on the ground

Direct observation and Indigenous tracking allow us to recover behavioral events that are difficult to capture using remote sensing alone.

Zimbabwe

Whole-group behavior

Whole-pride biologging provides a comparative window into movement, association and collective behavior in a higher-density ecological system.

Research questions

What I study

01 · Function

When is being social beneficial?

How ecological conditions alter the costs and benefits of cooperation, collective hunting, territoriality and other social strategies.

02 · Organization

How do animal societies hold together?

How cohesion, association and collective behavior are maintained, reorganized or lost as ecological conditions change.

03 · Mechanism

How do animals know what to do?

How sensory, cognitive, physiological and behavioral mechanisms allow individuals to adjust social behavior to local conditions.

Field science

How we study animals we cannot always see

My work combines natural-history observation with tools that capture movement, interaction and sensory information across scales.

Direct observation Indigenous tracking GPS biologging Accelerometry & magnetometry Bioacoustics Behavioral experiments

A collaborative programme: Genevieve E. Finerty and I co-lead a comparative African lion research programme and work with long-term scientific and field partners across multiple study systems.

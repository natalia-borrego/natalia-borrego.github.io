Teaching · mentorship · capacity building

Helping people become independent scientific thinkers.

My teaching is built around science as a way of thinking. I want students and early-career researchers to move beyond receiving information toward making predictions, weighing evidence, evaluating competing explanations, and taking ownership of questions they can answer themselves.

How I teach

Progressively increasing responsibility for scientific reasoning.

I use Bloom's taxonomy as a practical framework for course design. Students first establish conceptual foundations, then apply those ideas to unfamiliar cases, analyze evidence, evaluate competing explanations, and ultimately create arguments, projects, or research questions of their own.

Prediction → evidence → explanation

I structure activities around explicit predictions and the evidence that would support or challenge them. This shifts attention from memorizing the “right” answer to asking how we know what we know.

Multiple routes to participation

At the American University in Cairo, where many students were non-native English speakers, I used oral, written, visual, small-group, and low-stakes formats so participation did not depend on a single mode of communication.

Student research

From entry point to intellectual ownership.

I try to create tractable ways into real scientific problems. The goal is not simply to contribute labor to an established project, but to progressively take responsibility for the question, analysis, interpretation, and communication of the work.

Capacity building

Scientific participation beyond the classroom.

My commitment to access extends into field research and professional mentorship. I use collaborative research to create routes toward expertise, authorship, conference participation, postgraduate training, and independent scientific careers.

Field expertise as scientific expertise

In Botswana, San trackers reconstruct behavioral sequences from spoor that cannot be recovered from telemetry or direct observation alone. Their knowledge changes what questions can be answered, and field collaborators are recognized through scientific authorship where appropriate.

Trevor Balone progressed from field participation to coauthorship, conference presentation, professional certification, and postgraduate training.

ARTEMIS & early-career mentoring

Through the Max Planck ARTEMIS programme, I mentor early-career African researchers as they develop projects, strengthen research skills, and build independent trajectories. This work includes cross-cultural mentoring and hosting researchers through related development opportunities.
